# Competitive inspiration: reverse-engineer mechanics, not expression

## Audit
Evaluate reference channel/video in a separate lane: topic promise, first 1–5 sec, question/curiosity, narrative escalation, visual density, shot sizes, camera dynamics, action readability, graphics and text usage, sound, emotion, thumbnail-title synergy, ending, comments/share hypotheses. If uninspected, mark unknown and don't invent timestamps. Public views/likes show outcomes; don't assert causation. Use vidIQ/Nexlev only when connected and available, with provenance.

For a competitor **channel URL**, first identify 2–4 representative videos if actually accessible: recent recurring format, proven outlier, and ordinary baseline. Use different samples to distinguish the channel signature from a one-off viral hit. If no full video access, limit output to metadata-level hypotheses.

## Transfer matrix, required
For each mechanic:
- `REF_EVIDENCE` observed at time/source or `UNVERIFIED`;
- `MECHANIC`: abstract principle independent of original names, imagery or copyrighted assets;
- `WHY_EFFECTIVE`: a hypothesis, not verified retention;
- `DECISION`: KEEP_AS_PRINCIPLE | TRANSLATE_TO_BRAND | REJECT | UNKNOWN;
- `BRAND_NATIVE`: how executed within approved brand: graphics, voice, lens, grading, animation, hooks;
- `ORIGINALITY_CHANGE`: different premise, scene stakes, characters, geography, script, props and exact cut sequence;
- `COST_RISK`: simple/medium/high; model selection is downstream.

## Example
Competitor: dramatic first-person time travel vlog. Reusable mechanic: direct first-person address during an imminent historical event and dramatic irony from knowing the outcome. Your implementation: original future traveler/personality with own world-building, channel typography/palette, unique event and story reveal, deliberate POV camera, costume and authored dialogue. Do not recreate their scenes, wardrobe, channel logo, exact words, or distinctive sequence. If brand is cinematic fantasy rather than first-person vlog, transfer 'intimate presenter + foreknowledge' with a motivated camera inside brand range, not competitor's whole visual identity.

## Prevent aesthetic hijacking
Ask at each shot: 'If we replaced the logo, would this clearly be the competitor video?' If yes, deepen original story, production design, grade, recurring brand motif, sound and camera purpose — don't just change color filter.

## Multi-reference
If several competitors: label each source; pick best mechanism per goal and reject mutually contradictory grammar. Never mix unsourced observations. Own-brand constraints remain authoritative.
