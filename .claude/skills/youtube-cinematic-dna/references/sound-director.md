# SOUND & HUMAN PERFORMANCE DIRECTOR

## Build audio from spatial causality
Separate: `dialogue/narration`, `room tone/ambience`, `primary action sound`, `foley`, `distant offscreen`, `music`, `transitions`. The mix must reflect camera perspective (inside car vs curb vs helicopter), acoustic space, distance and occlusion. No magical constant-volume sirens across cuts.

## Voice actor direction
For every spoken line specify `character, goal/subtext, physical state, scene environment, beat timing, emotion trajectory, pace, breaths, emphasis, conversational style`. Use human performance or licensed synthetic voice with stable identity. Avoid mechanically uniform TTS cadence, inappropriate pronunciation, over-perfect announcer delivery, breathlessness mismatched to action and out-of-sync lip motion.

## Foley / environment
If motion appears, define physical sync: wheel/tire/gear/suspension, footsteps surface-dependent, cloth/prop contact, impacts with attack/body/debris/tail, weather/wind with perspective, neighboring crowd/traffic offscreen. Natural sound first, cinematic enhancement selectively after. Sound bridges/J-cuts/L-cuts support film geography.

## Music
Track only energy/emotion, rhythmic cues, rise/withdrawal/payoff; do not use unlicensed tracks. Maintain speech intelligibility, avoid endless full-volume music or whooshes on every cut. Use real rights-clear sound libraries or independently generated licensed cues.

## Acceptance (real audio file required)
Audio-only pass for intelligibility, believable acoustics and natural pacing; synchrony for foley/dialogue, matched timbre over cuts, absence of repeated loops/clipping, purposeful dynamic range and clean delivered mix. A transcript alone cannot verify this. Do not invent loudness readings without measuring.
