Why is Australia not an island, but a continent, while Greenland is "only" an island? 🌏
On many world maps Greenland even looks bigger than Australia. Slide it down to Australia and it shrinks: Australia is about 3.5 times bigger. There is no official rule for what counts as a continent, but geologists look at the crust underneath: Australia sits on its own plate, the Australian Plate, while Greenland sits on the North American Plate. That is why Greenland is usually called the world's largest island and Australia the smallest continent (some sources, like Guinness, also call Australia the largest "continental island": the terms are debated).
