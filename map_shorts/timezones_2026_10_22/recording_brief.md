# Recording brief: "Which Country Has the Most Time Zones?" (human narrator)

Language: English (US or neutral). Length: about 68 s at 155 words per minute. Voice: friendly, curious, confident teacher; smile in the voice; no news-reader tone.

## Technical
- Quiet room, soft furnishings, microphone 15-20 cm from the mouth, pop filter or a sock over the mic.
- WAV 48 kHz, 24-bit (or 16-bit), mono, no noise reduction, no compression, no effects. Peaks around -6 dB.
- Record the whole text in one take, then a second take. Leave 2 seconds of silence at the start and the end. Do not edit out breaths.
- If a line goes wrong: stop, wait one second, repeat the whole line. Do not restart the file.

## Script with direction (read the lines in order; "/" = short pause 0.3 s)
1. Which country has the most time zones? / (real question, rising at the end)
2. Easy, right? Or maybe not. / (first half confident, "Or maybe not" slower, with a smirk)
3. Russia is huge, so it must win. It spans eleven time zones, from Kaliningrad all the way to Kamchatka. / (like you are sure)
4. The United States has eleven too, once you count Alaska, Hawaii and its islands. /
5. So the winner is Russia, or the United States. But that's wrong. / ("But that's wrong." short, flat, a half-second pause before it)
6. Fine, the winner is Monaco, with its one time zone. I'm joking. / ("I'm joking." quick, light, laugh in the voice)
7. The real winner is France. / (slow, a beat of silence before "France")
8. Mainland France uses a single time, the same as Berlin. /
9. But France also owns islands and territories all around the world. / (building up)
10. In the Caribbean, in South America, in the Indian Ocean, in the Pacific, and it even claims a slice of Antarctica. / (list rhythm, one breath, slight lift on each place)
11. Every one of them keeps its own clock. /
12. From French Polynesia, ten hours behind Greenwich, to Wallis and Futuna, twelve hours ahead. /
13. That makes twelve time zones, or thirteen if you count the Antarctic claim. /
14. A country whose capital sits in one zone, and whose clocks cover the whole planet. / (amazed)
15. So which country has the most time zones? France. / (repeat of line 1, then a firm "France.")

## Pronunciation
Kaliningrad (kah-LEE-neen-grad), Kamchatka (kam-CHAT-ka), Wallis and Futuna (WAH-lis and foo-TOO-na), Greenwich (GREN-itch), Polynesia (pol-ih-NEE-zha).

## Delivery
Send the WAV to the person who builds the video. The animation is timed to your recording (forced alignment), so please do not change the wording.
