Vice City, USA. The darkest side of the sunniest place in America. That is how Rockstar sells it. But almost every street in this trailer has a real address. Today we put the game next to the real Miami, place by place, and see how much of it actually exists.
Quick rule before we start. Rockstar has never published a list of real places. So everything here is either named on official material, or spotted by fans and matched to a real map. I will tell you which is which.
Let's start with the big picture. The state is called Leonida. Rockstar has named six regions: Vice City, the Leonida Keys, Grassrivers, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga National Park. Put that next to a map of Florida, and the shape speaks for itself. A chain of islands to the south, like the Florida Keys. A huge swamp, like the Everglades. And one big city on the Atlantic coast.
Even the county is a wink. Fan wikis place Vice City in Vice-Dale County. In real life, Miami sits in Miami-Dade.
Chapter one. Ocean Beach.
Rockstar's own description talks about pastel art deco hotels and bright white sands in Ocean Beach. In real life, that is South Beach and Ocean Drive. The Miami Beach Architectural District has around eight hundred historic buildings. It joined the National Register of Historic Places on May fourteenth, nineteen seventy-nine. Most of them were built in the nineteen thirties and forties: rounded corners, racing stripes, porthole windows and neon at night.
Those buildings almost disappeared. In the seventies, developers wanted to knock them down. A small group of locals fought back, and the neon you see in the trailer exists because they won.
Chapter two. The port.
Rockstar calls VC Port the cruise ship capital of the world. Here is the funny part: that is real. PortMiami uses almost the same slogan, cruise capital of the world. On the satellite you can see the island, right between downtown and the beach, with the giant ships lined up along it.
Next to it runs the MacArthur Causeway, about three and a half miles of road over open water, first opened in nineteen twenty. It is the road you see in so many GTA shots: towers on one end, beach on the other.
Chapter three. Tequesta.
A road sign in an official screenshot points to Tequesta. Fans match it to Brickell, the glass financial district south of the Miami River. And the name is not random. The Tequesta were the people who lived at the mouth of the Miami River long before the city.
In nineteen ninety-eight, workers cleared a lot in Brickell for new towers. Under the ground, archaeologist Robert Carr's team found a perfect circle cut into the rock, thirty-eight feet across, with twenty-four holes. It is called the Miami Circle. It may be up to two thousand years old, and it is now a National Historic Landmark. So the GTA neighborhood is named after the people who carved it.
Today Brickell is the opposite: one of the biggest financial districts in the country, packed with high-rises, and around forty thousand residents.
Chapter four. Peacock Bay.
On an exit sign in the second trailer, at thirty-five seconds, you can read Peacock Bay. Miami has a Peacock Park, in Coconut Grove. It is named after the Peacock family, who opened the Bay View House around eighteen eighty-two. It was the first hotel on Biscayne Bay. The building was torn down in nineteen twenty-six, but the name stayed. And Rockstar picked exactly that one.
Chapter five. Tisha-Wocka.
Rockstar's own copy mentions the Tisha-Wocka flea market and its bootleg brands. Say it out loud, and you hear Opa-locka. Opa-locka is a small city north of Miami, founded in nineteen twenty-six by aviation pioneer Glenn Curtiss. He built it like a scene from the Arabian Nights: domes, minarets and arches. Its City Hall still looks like a palace, and around twenty of its buildings are on the National Register. And yes, there is a real, famous flea market in that area too.
Chapter six. Little Cuba.
The official description talks about the bustling panaderias of Little Cuba. Panaderias are bakeries. In Miami, that is Little Havana. After nineteen fifty-nine, hundreds of thousands of Cuban exiles settled around Calle Ocho, Eighth Street. Since the early seventies, older men have played dominoes in Maximo Gomez Park, which everybody calls Domino Park. If the game gets the smell of cafecito and the click of dominoes right, that street will feel alive.
Chapter seven. Stockyard.
A sign in the first trailer reads Stockyard. Fans believe it is Wynwood, an old district of warehouses. In two thousand nine, developer Tony Goldman opened the Wynwood Walls and invited street artists to paint them. Now the whole district is an open-air gallery. The game's colorful walls look a lot like it, but remember, this one is a fan match, not a confirmation.
Chapter eight. The landmarks.
The arena by the bay, with its white curved roof, looks a lot like the Kaseya Center. It opened in nineteen ninety-nine and seats nearly twenty thousand for basketball. The little driverless train between the towers looks like the Metromover. It opened in nineteen eighty-six, and it has been free to ride since two thousand two. And the road jumping from island to island looks like the Venetian Causeway: twelve bridges, two of them drawbridges.
Chapter nine. Beyond the city.
Leave Vice City and drive south, and you hit the Leonida Keys: tiny islands tied together by long bridges over open sea. The real Florida Keys work exactly like that. The Overseas Highway runs about one hundred thirteen miles from the mainland to Key West, across forty-two bridges. The longest is the Seven Mile Bridge. Its old twin was built for Henry Flagler's railroad, finished in nineteen twelve, and a hurricane in nineteen thirty-five ended the trains for good.
West of the city lies Grassrivers, the game's swamp. In real life, that is the Everglades, a national park since nineteen forty-seven, covering about one and a half million acres. It is the only place on Earth where alligators and crocodiles live side by side. The first trailer even shows an alligator wandering into a store. Florida has had real headlines just like that.
So, how real is Vice City? Here is the count. Ocean Beach, VC Port, Tisha-Wocka and Little Cuba are named in official text, and each has a clear real twin. Tequesta and Peacock Bay appear on official signs and match real names. The Keys and Grassrivers are official regions with obvious twins. Stockyard and the landmarks are fan matches, but the satellite makes a strong case.
One more thing. Real Miami was founded on July twenty-eighth, eighteen ninety-six. It is often called the only major American city founded by a woman, Julia Tuttle. She convinced the same railroad tycoon, Henry Flagler, to bring his trains south. Without that railroad, there would be no Miami, and maybe no Vice City either.
