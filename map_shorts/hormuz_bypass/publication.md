## Description
For months the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow exit of the Persian Gulf, has been almost shut. Before 2026 roughly a fifth of the world's oil passed through it. So the United Arab Emirates is leaning on a second coast: pipelines, ports and a railway that run through the desert and the Hajar Mountains to Fujairah, outside the strait. 🗺️🚆🛢️

In this video: why Hormuz is such a bottleneck, what happened to shipping in 2026, how the Habshan-Fujairah pipeline works, why a second pipeline is being built, how Khor Fakkan and Fujairah took over container traffic, what Etihad Rail's new Fujairah freight service does, and why none of this fully replaces the sea.

Facts and sources (figures are approximate and can change; check the links)
- Hormuz ~20% of global oil before March 2026; traffic fell from ~95 to ~10 commodity ships a day after March 1 (AFP analysis of Kpler data, reported by Iraqi News, 10 Oct 2026).
- Habshan-Fujairah pipeline: design capacity ~1.5 million bpd, up to ~1.8 (CNBC, Everhem, May 2026). Second pipeline ~50% built in May 2026, start expected 2027, combined ~3-3.4 million bpd (CNBC, The National).
- Khor Fakkan: one report 2,000 -> 50,000 containers/week (The National, 17 May 2026); another gives other figures.
- Etihad Rail / AD Ports Fujairah-ICAD freight service, ~200 km, three times a week, launched Sept 2026 (RailFreight, 25 Sep 2026). The companies have not said Hormuz was the reason.
- Routes on the maps are simplified. Stock footage is illustrative and not filmed in the Gulf. Seven scenes are AI-generated illustrations (labelled on screen): strait with tankers, anchored tankers, sailor on deck, oil port, desert pipeline, train in mountains, container ship at sunset.
