## Description
Madagascar is only about 400 km from Africa, yet it has no lions, zebras or giraffes. The island split from Africa roughly 180 million years ago, long before lions existed, so its animals evolved alone: over 90 % of its wildlife is found nowhere else. Scientists think the ancestors of lemurs crossed the sea on rafts of vegetation about 60 million years ago. Its top predator, the fossa, looks like a cat but is not one. 🦎🌍

Numbers (approximate, from Wikipedia): Mozambique Channel 419 km across at its narrowest; Madagascar split from Africa during the Early Jurassic (about 180 million years ago) and from India about 90 million years ago; oldest lion fossils up to about 2 million years old; lemur ancestors reached Madagascar roughly 62-65 million years ago (rafting is the most accepted explanation); the fossa is Madagascar's largest mammalian carnivore.
