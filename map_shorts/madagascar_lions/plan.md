## Concept
A map shows Africa and Madagascar. Left: African animals (lion, zebra, giraffe). Right: Madagascar's animals (lemur, chameleon, fossa). The gap is only 419 km, but the island split off about 180 million years ago, long before lions existed (about 2 million years). Its animals evolved alone: over 90 % of the wildlife is found nowhere else. Lemurs got there by floating on rafts of vegetation about 60 million years ago. The top predator is the fossa, which looks like a cat but is not one. Comic payoff: a lemur cruises on its raft, a lion waves from the shore.

2. "This island is full of animals found nowhere else on Earth."

## Fact check (Wikipedia, read 2026-10-15)
- Madagascar split from Africa during the Early Jurassic, "around 180 million years ago", and from the Indian subcontinent "approximately 90 million years ago" (Wikipedia "Madagascar"). Note: an earlier draft said 160; the page says 180.
- Mozambique Channel: "419 km across at its narrowest point" (Wikipedia "Mozambique Channel") -> script says "just four hundred kilometers away".
- "over 90 % of its wildlife being endemic" (Madagascar).
- Lions: oldest lion-like fossils "up to 2 million years old" (Olduvai); Panthera lineage 10.8 Ma (Wikipedia "Lion") -> "Lions appeared only two million years ago" is the fossil date of recognisable lions; the genus is older. Wording stays "lions".
- Lemurs: endemic to Madagascar; ancestors from Africa about 62-65 Ma; rafting is "the most accepted explanation"; the channel was at least about 560 km wide then (Wikipedia "Lemur"). Script "Lemurs floated there on rafts of vegetation, sixty million years ago" is the accepted hypothesis, not proven; description says "scientists think".
- Fossa: "the largest mammalian carnivore on Madagascar"; not a cat and not a mongoose; belongs to Eupleridae; cat-like look from convergent evolution (Wikipedia "Fossa").
- Not checked on Wikipedia, common knowledge, verify before publishing: no native zebras and giraffes on Madagascar (only no native big cats confirmed by absence in the sources).
- Wording rule: say "scientists think" in the description for rafting; never "proved".
