## VO (86 words, Kokoro 34.1-35.6 s)
In 1892, this country lived the same day twice.
American traders convinced Samoa's king to switch to the American date, so Monday, July fourth, happened two times.
But over a century later, its business had shifted to New Zealand, Australia and Asia, and Samoa was a full day behind them.
So in 2011, it jumped back across the date line and deleted Friday, December thirtieth.
People went to bed on Thursday and woke up on Saturday.
And if your birthday was that Friday? Sorry. Business first.

## Checks (done)
- 1892: King Malietoa Laupepa persuaded by American traders to adopt the American date; Monday 4 July 1892 repeated (Wikipedia, International Date Line).
- 2011: Friday 30 December 2011 removed; Thursday 29 -> Saturday 31 (Wikipedia, Time in Samoa / IDL).
- Reason 2011: PM Tuilaepa: improve business with New Zealand, Australia, China and Asia (Wikipedia, Time in Samoa).
- Today Samoa UTC+13, American Samoa UTC-11: 24 h apart (not used in VO to keep the length).
- 1892 -> 2011 = 119 years -> "over a century later" OK.
- No portrait of the real king (hands only) to avoid misrepresenting a real person.
