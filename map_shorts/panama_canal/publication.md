## Description
Every day, ships longer than three football fields line up off Panama to pay for a shortcut just 82 km long. The biggest pay close to a million dollars a crossing, and some have bid almost four million just to skip the queue. 🚢💰🌧️

We follow one giant container ship from the Atlantic to the Pacific: the anchorage at Colón, the compulsory pilot, the giant locks that lift ships 26 meters with nothing but gravity, Gatun Lake, the Culebra Cut, and the 2023 drought that showed the canal's real currency is rain.

Facts and sources (approximate; check the links)
- Canal length ~82 km; opened 15 Aug 1914; handed to Panama 31 Dec 1999; expanded locks opened 26 June 2016 (Wikipedia, "Panama Canal").
- French effort 1881–1889, ~22,000 deaths (estimate); US construction cost almost $500 million, ~5,600 deaths (Wikipedia).
- Neopanamax chambers 427 × 55 × 18.3 m; water-saving basins reuse ~60% of water per lockage (Panama Canal Authority).
- Ships lifted ~26 m to Gatun Lake; the lake was the largest artificial lake in the world when built (Wikipedia).
- ~200 million litres of fresh water per transit through the old locks (Wikipedia).
- FY2025: ~13,400 transits, revenue ~$5.7 billion (Panama Canal Authority).
- Slot auctions: $3.975 million (Nov 2023); a near-$4 million bid (Aug 2026) (Reuters / trade press).
- 2023 drought: ~40% less rain, draft and daily-slot limits (ACP). Río Indio reservoir planned (ACP).
- Average transit ~11 h between the outer locks (ACP statistics). Richard Halliburton swam the canal in 1928 and paid 36 cents.
- Routes on the maps are simplified. Ship scenes and 1880s–1920s "archival-look" scenes are AI-generated illustrations and are labelled on screen; they are not real footage.
