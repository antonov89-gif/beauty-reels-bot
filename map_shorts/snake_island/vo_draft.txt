Brazil banned people from this island.
It's called Snake Island. It sits thirty-three kilometers off the coast of São Paulo, and it's only forty-three hectares.
You may have heard there's one snake on every square meter. Or even four hundred thousand snakes. That's a myth.
A 2021 study counted about twenty-four hundred to twenty-nine hundred golden lanceheads. Still thousands, on a rock the size of sixty football fields.
How did they get there? Thousands of years ago, rising seas cut the island off from the mainland. The snakes were trapped.
With no mammals to hunt, they learned to catch birds. A bird can fly away, so the venom has to work fast. One study suggested it's five times stronger than its mainland cousin.
Nobody lives there now. A lighthouse went up in 1909, and the last people left when it became automatic in the 1920s.
Today only the Brazilian Navy and approved scientists may land. Everyone else is banned.
But there's another reason. In 2008, illegal buyers reportedly offered up to thirty thousand dollars for these snakes.
And that matters, because the golden lancehead is critically endangered. Fewer than three thousand are left.
So the island that's dangerous to visit is also the snake's last refuge.
A beautiful beach. A green rainforest. Zero tourist reviews.
