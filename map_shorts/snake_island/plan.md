Same family as Samoa/Kiribati: an absurd true claim in the first line, a myth busted with a number, a cause, a human-scale detail, a money twist, a comic last line.

## Concept
Brazil closed a 43-hectare island 33 km off São Paulo. The old myth says one snake per square metre (even 430,000 snakes); scientists counted about 2,400-2,900 golden lanceheads. Rising seas isolated them thousands of years ago; with no mammals they learned to catch birds, and the venom became fast. People left in the 1920s when the lighthouse was automated; today only the Navy and approved researchers land. Twist: illegal buyers reportedly offered up to $30,000 in 2008 and the snake is critically endangered, so the ban is also protection. Comic last line: "Zero tourist reviews."

## Voice-over (215 words; Kokoro test 93.8 s with lead + tail; Lumean about the same or a little shorter)
1. Brazil banned people from this island.
2. It's called Snake Island. It sits thirty-three kilometers off the coast of São Paulo, and it's only forty-three hectares.
3. You may have heard there's one snake on every square meter. Or even four hundred thousand snakes. That's a myth.
4. A 2021 study counted about twenty-four hundred to twenty-nine hundred golden lanceheads. Still thousands, on a rock the size of sixty football fields.
5. How did they get there? Thousands of years ago, rising seas cut the island off from the mainland. The snakes were trapped.
6. With no mammals to hunt, they learned to catch birds. A bird can fly away, so the venom has to work fast. One study suggested it's five times stronger than its mainland cousin.
7. Nobody lives there now. A lighthouse went up in 1909, and the last people left when it became automatic in the 1920s.
8. Today only the Brazilian Navy and approved scientists may land. Everyone else is banned.
9. But there's another reason. In 2008, illegal buyers reportedly offered up to thirty thousand dollars for these snakes.
10. And that matters, because the golden lancehead is critically endangered. Fewer than three thousand are left.
11. So the island that's dangerous to visit is also the snake's last refuge.
12. A beautiful beach. A green rainforest. Zero tourist reviews.
If Lumean runs over ~95 s, drop line 7's date detail or merge lines 10 and 11.

## Fact check (Wikipedia "Ilha da Queimada Grande" and "Golden lancehead", read 2026-10-15)
- Area about 430,000 m² (43 ha); about 33 km off the coast of São Paulo state; administered by ICMBio (Chico Mendes Institute). 43 ha = about 60 football fields (430,000 / 7,140 m²).
- Old claim of about 430,000 snakes (unsourced); a 2021 study (Abrahão et al.) estimated about 2,414 (2D scan) to 2,899 (3D scan); the Snake Island page's older "2,000 to 4,000" matches. The "one snake per square metre" figure is NOT addressed by Wikipedia; the VO says "you may have heard" and calls it a myth because 43 ha would hold 430,000 snakes at that density (the unsourced "430,000" is the same myth). Keep wording "myth", not "debunked by X".
- Isolation: connected to the mainland during the last glacial period, isolated by sea-level rise at the start of the Holocene; the page gives no date, so the VO says "thousands of years ago".
- No mammals; prey are ectotherms, arthropods and migrating birds; holding prey in the mouth is "thought to be" an adaptation to bird hunting (hypothesis). Venom is "five times as potent as B. jararaca" and the "fastest-acting in the genus" according to one 1995 study (Duarte et al.); the VO says "One study suggested...". Do not say it is the deadliest snake or claim human deaths: Wikipedia says there is no official report of a human bite.
- Lighthouse built in 1909; last people left when it was automated in the 1920s; access limited to the Brazilian Navy and researchers vetted by ICMBio ("Travel to the island is strictly prohibited").
- Critically endangered (IUCN); illegal-trade offers of up to $30,000 in 2008 (the page does not say per snake, so the VO says "reportedly offered up to thirty thousand dollars for these snakes", which should be softened to "for them" if a source for per-snake pricing is not found).
- The Wikipedia article is tagged as lacking inline citations; the legend of a keeper family killed by snakes is NOT used.
- Respect rule: no mockery of any people; no humans shown near danger.
