## Description
In March 2021 a 400-metre container ship got wedged across the Suez Canal after a sandstorm and blocked about 12% of world trade for six days. Tugs, excavators and dredgers could not free it until a supermoon's extra-high tide helped more than a dozen tugboats pull it loose. Delayed goods were estimated at about $400 million per hour. The ship's owner reportedly settled for $540 million and a tugboat. 🚢🌕

Numbers (approximate, from Wikipedia): grounded 23 March 2021, freed 29 March (6 days 7 hours); length 400 m; about 12% of global trade passes through the canal; Lloyd's List estimate of about $400 million per hour (about $9.6 billion of goods a day); fuel and about 9,000 tonnes of ballast water removed, an excavator and suction dredgers dug at the bow; 14 tugs in the final pull (sources differ, one page says 8); settlement reported at $540 million plus a 75-ton tugboat.
Sources: Wikipedia "2021 Suez Canal obstruction", "Ever Given".
