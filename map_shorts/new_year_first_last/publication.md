## Description
Which place on Earth welcomes the New Year first, and which one is last? 🎆 It all comes down to the International Date Line 🗺️

The first to hit midnight is Kiribati 🇰🇮, about 120,000 people on 33 islands. On Kiritimati Island, villages called London, Banana and Poland celebrate before anyone else. Until 1995 the line split Kiribati so its capital and eastern islands lived a day apart, so the country moved its time zone and the line now bends far to the east.

Then midnight sweeps west through New Zealand, Australia, Asia, Europe and Africa to the Americas 🌎 One of the last inhabited places is American Samoa 🇦🇸, only about 70 km from Samoa 🇼🇸, which is a full day ahead. And the very last spots, Baker and Howland Islands, have no people at all, just seabirds 🐦🥂

## Fact notes
- Kiribati Line Islands UTC+14 since 1 Jan 1995 (first). Population ~120k (2020 census 119,940); 33 islands.
- Paris village on Kiritimati is abandoned, so it was left out.
- American Samoa (UTC-11) shares last inhabited midnight with Niue, so the script says "one of the last".
- Samoa UTC+13 since 2011 date-line switch; no DST since 2021, so the gap is 24 h (the reference says 25 h, outdated).
- Baker and Howland: UTC-12, uninhabited.
- Date line drawn as a simplified path.
